\section{Agentic RL: Multi-Turn, Tool-Using, Long-Horizon Agents}\label{sec:agentic}

\paragraph{From a bandit to a POMDP}
\citet{zhang2025landscape} define agentic RL by contrast: the LLM RL of Section~\ref{sec:background} is a degenerate single-step MDP (prompt, one completion, scalar reward), whereas agents act in a temporally extended POMDP. A trajectory is $\tau=(q,a_1,o_1,a_2,o_2,\dots,a_T)$, where each action $a_k$ is a \emph{turn}, \ie a block of generated tokens that ends in a tool call or a final answer, and $o_k$ is the observation returned by the tool or environment. The environment state (interpreter variables, a file system, a web page) is hidden; the policy sees only the history $h_k=(q,a_{<k},o_{<k})$, and reward usually arrives once, as $R(\tau)$. Two time scales are nested: $T$ turns, each of $|a_k|$ tokens. Observation tokens are not sampled from $\pi_\theta$: they are off-policy inputs that condition every later turn. Applied to $\tau$, the GRPO advantage (Eq.~\ref{eq:grpo-adv}) gives every generated token of every turn the same value. Credit assignment thus has two levels, \emph{which turn} and \emph{which tokens within it}; this survey focuses on the first.

\paragraph{Task families and horizons}
Table~\ref{tab:task-families} lists the main families. Horizons span about three orders of magnitude: tool-integrated reasoning (TIR) for math and search QA use a handful of turns, text games and web navigation tens, SWE agents tens to a hundred, and research agents hundreds, with MiroThinker reaching up to 600 tool calls per task \citep{miromind2025mirothinker} and one 2026 harness environment allowing up to 1{,}000 steps\unverified. The common thread is the reward: almost every 2025 system is trained on outcome-only, trajectory-level reward, typically final-answer correctness plus a format term. Exceptions are rare: StepSearch \citep{wang2025stepsearch} adds per-step information-gain rewards\unverified, and R1-Searcher \citep{song2025r1} and verl-agent/GiGPO \citep{feng2025group} combine outcome and process signals. Math TIR, the setting of the ToolCredit study (Section~\ref{sec:toolcredit}), is the shortest-horizon tier: the cleanest testbed for turn-level credit, and also the main threat to the external validity of any result obtained there.

\paragraph{What the 2025 tricks fix}
\emph{Stability fixes:} tool and retrieval tokens are masked out of the loss, a practice introduced for search agents \citep{jin2025search}\unverified. Masking is not sufficient: in SimpleTIR \citep{xue2025simpletir}, low-probability tool-output tokens fed back as input compound across turns into gradient-norm explosions; dropping trajectories with \emph{void turns} (neither code nor a final answer) from the update lifts Qwen2.5-7B on AIME24 from 22.1 to 50.5. RAGEN \citep{wang2025ragen} names the multi-turn failure mode the \emph{Echo Trap} (reward-variance collapse, entropy drop, gradient spikes) and counters it with variance-based trajectory filtering, critic baselining and decoupled clipping (StarPO-S). ARPO \citep{dong2025agentic} and AEPO \citep{dong2025agenticentropy} branch rollouts where entropy spikes after tool calls. \emph{Horizon and data fixes:} fully asynchronous rollouts keep long trajectories from stalling a batch (\citealp{gao2025beyond,fu2025areal}); context management keeps only recent tool responses so that 256K tokens can hold 600 calls \citep{miromind2025mirothinker}; and synthetic hard tasks supply learnable signal \citep{li2025websailor}. None of these recipes decides \emph{which turn deserves the credit}: filtering (void turns, StarPO-S, DAPO-style zero-variance filtering) changes which trajectories enter the update, not how the outcome is attributed within them (ARPO's attribution over shared and branched segments is a partial exception\unverified).

\paragraph{Frameworks}
veRL/HybridFlow \citep{sheng2025hybridflow} is the default academic substrate; alternatives motivated by long-tailed tool latency include OpenRLHF \citep{hu2024openrlhf}, AReaL \citep{fu2025areal}, ROLL \citep{wang2025reinforcement} and Agent Lightning \citep{luo2025agent}. What credit assignment needs is rarely a headline feature: grouping by turn, keeping turn boundaries in the batch, and branching from intermediate states.

\paragraph{Can the environment be reset to an intermediate state?}
This question decides which credit methods are feasible at all. Monte-Carlo turn values, sibling baselines and counterfactual ``redo this turn'' estimates all restart from the state after turn $k$. In 2025 stacks the only general mechanism is \emph{prefix replay} (re-execute the stored prefix's tool calls), exact only for deterministic, stateless tools. Math with a Python interpreter is nearly stateless (fresh sandboxes, or state rebuilt by replaying earlier cells), so branching from any turn costs little more than extra generation. SWE, terminal, OS and web agents are not: their state lives in containers, file systems or the live web (which is also non-stationary, hence ZeroSearch's simulated engine; \citealp{sun2025zerosearch}). Resets there need checkpoint/restore infrastructure, which arrived as a 2026 cluster. Branching Policy Optimization (BPO; \citealp{bpo2026branching}) snapshots the sandbox at branch points to build tree-structured rollouts whose siblings serve as baselines, and states the assumption of \emph{snapshot fidelity} (restoring yields an identical transition distribution). DeltaBox \citep{deltabox2026} provides millisecond-level checkpoint/rollback and argues that with tens to hundreds of branches, fork latency bounds training throughput. Crab \citep{crab2026} forks from checkpoints instead of re-executing shared prefixes, citing Tree-GRPO-style workloads \citep{ji2025tree}; workspace forking (ForkRL) and per-turn SWE snapshots (SPROUT) follow the same direction\unverified. Rollout branching has thus become a credit estimator in its own right, with a cost that ranges from nearly free (math+Python) to infrastructure-bound (SWE, web).

\paragraph{Where credit becomes the bottleneck}
Once stability is under control, the remaining question is which turn caused the outcome. This is the stated motivation of the 2025 turn-level methods, MT-GRPO \citep{wei2025reinforcing}, GiGPO \citep{feng2025group}, SPA-RL \citep{wang2025spa} and Tree-GRPO \citep{ji2025tree}, and of roughly a dozen 2026 preprints on leave-one-turn, information-gain and rubric-attributed turn rewards\unverified. They form a crowded field with no standard benchmark or comparison protocol; Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy} organizes them.
