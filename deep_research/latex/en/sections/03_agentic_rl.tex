\section{Agentic RL: Multi-Turn, Tool-Using, Long-Horizon Agents}\label{sec:agentic}

\paragraph{From a bandit to a POMDP}
\citet{zhang2025landscape} define agentic RL by contrast: LLM RL in the style of Section~\ref{sec:background} is a degenerate single-step MDP (prompt, one completion, scalar reward), whereas agents act in a temporally extended POMDP. Concretely, a trajectory is $\tau=(q,a_1,o_1,a_2,o_2,\dots,a_K)$, where each action $a_k$ is a \emph{turn}, \ie a block of generated tokens that ends in a tool call or a final answer, and $o_k$ is the observation returned by the tool or environment. The environment's internal state $e_k$ (interpreter variables, a file system, a browser page, the live web) is hidden; the policy conditions only on the history $h_k=(q,a_{<k},o_{<k})$. Reward usually arrives once, as $R(\tau)$ at the end. Two time scales are therefore nested: $K$ turns, each containing $|a_k|$ tokens. The observation tokens sit in the context but are not sampled from $\pi_\theta$, so they are off-policy inputs that nonetheless condition every later turn. Applied to $\tau$, the GRPO advantage \eqref{eq:grpo-adv} gives every generated token of every turn the same value. Credit assignment thus has two levels, \emph{which turn} and \emph{which tokens within it}; this survey focuses on the first.

\paragraph{Task families and horizons}
Table~\ref{tab:task-families} lists the main families. Horizons span about three orders of magnitude: tool-integrated reasoning (TIR) for math and search QA use a handful of turns, text games and web navigation tens, SWE agents tens to a hundred, and frontier research agents hundreds, with MiroThinker reaching up to 600 tool calls per task \citep{miromind2025mirothinker} and one 2026 harness environment allowing up to 1{,}000 steps\unverified. The common thread is the reward: almost every 2025 system is trained on outcome-only, trajectory-level reward, typically final-answer correctness plus a format term. Exceptions are rare: StepSearch \citep{wang2025stepsearch} adds per-step information-gain rewards\unverified, and R1-Searcher \citep{song2025r1} and verl-agent/GiGPO \citep{feng2025group} combine outcome and process signals. Math TIR, the setting of our own case study, is the shortest-horizon tier. This makes it the cleanest testbed for turn-level credit, and also the main threat to the external validity of any result obtained there.

\paragraph{What the 2025 tricks fix}
The recipes that made multi-turn RL work fall into two groups. \emph{Stability fixes:} tool and retrieval tokens are masked out of the loss, a practice introduced for search agents \citep{jin2025search}\unverified. Masking is necessary but not sufficient: SimpleTIR \citep{xue2025simpletir} shows that tool outputs still cause distributional drift, because low-probability tokens fed back as input compound across turns and produce gradient-norm explosions. Its fix filters trajectories that contain \emph{void turns} (neither a code block nor a final answer) out of the policy update while keeping them in the advantage computation, which lifts Qwen2.5-7B on AIME24 from 22.1 to 50.5. RAGEN \citep{wang2025ragen} names the multi-turn failure mode the \emph{Echo Trap} (reward-variance collapse, entropy drop, gradient spikes) and counters it with StarPO-S: variance-based trajectory filtering, critic baselining and decoupled clipping. ARPO \citep{dong2025agentic} and AEPO \citep{dong2025agenticentropy} build rollout and optimization around the entropy spikes that follow tool calls, branching more where the model is uncertain. \emph{Horizon and data fixes:} fully asynchronous rollouts keep long trajectories from stalling a batch (ASearcher on AReaL; \citealp{gao2025beyond,fu2025areal}); context management keeps only the most recent tool responses so that 256K tokens can hold 600 calls \citep{miromind2025mirothinker}; and synthetic hard tasks supply learnable signal \citep{li2025websailor}. None of these recipes decides \emph{which turn deserves the credit}. Filtering in particular (void turns, StarPO-S, DAPO-style zero-variance filtering) changes which trajectories enter the update, not how the outcome is attributed within them. ARPO's attribution over shared and branched segments is a partial exception\unverified.

\paragraph{Frameworks}
veRL/HybridFlow \citep{sheng2024hybridflow} is the default academic substrate (Search-R1, ReTool, SimpleTIR, RAGEN, verl-agent and AgentGym-RL build on it). Its alternatives are motivated mainly by the long-tailed latency of tool calls and long contexts. They include OpenRLHF \citep{hu2024openrlhf}, the fully asynchronous AReaL \citep{fu2025areal}, ROLL \citep{wang2025reinforcement}, Agent Lightning \citep{luo2025agent}, which decouples agent execution from training, and slime, SkyRL and rLLM. For credit assignment, the relevant capabilities are rarely the headline features: grouping by turn, retaining per-turn boundaries in the batch, and branching from intermediate states.

\paragraph{Can the environment be reset to an intermediate state?}
Whether an environment can be reset to an intermediate state decides which credit methods are feasible at all. Monte-Carlo turn values, sibling baselines and counterfactual ``re-do this turn'' estimates all require restarting from the state after turn $k$, not only from $s_0$. In 2025 mainstream stacks the only general mechanism is \emph{prefix replay}: regenerate from the stored token prefix and re-execute its tool calls. This is exact only when tools are deterministic and stateless. The setting of our case study, math with a Python interpreter, is nearly stateless: code cells run in fresh or short-lived sandboxes, or their state can be rebuilt by replaying earlier cells, so branching from any turn costs little more than extra generation. SWE, terminal, OS and web agents are not stateless. Their state lives in containers, file systems, databases or the live web, and live search is in addition non-stationary, which is why ZeroSearch \citep{sun2025zerosearch} substitutes an LLM-simulated engine. For these settings true intermediate resets needed dedicated checkpoint/restore infrastructure, which arrived as a 2026 cluster. Branching Policy Optimization \citep{bpo2026branching} snapshots the sandbox at branch points to build tree-structured rollouts whose siblings serve as baselines, and it states explicitly the assumption of \emph{snapshot fidelity} (restoring yields an identical transition distribution). DeltaBox \citep{deltabox2026} provides millisecond-level checkpoint/rollback and argues that with tens to hundreds of branches, fork latency bounds training throughput. Crab \citep{crab2026} forks from checkpoints instead of re-executing shared prefixes, citing Tree-GRPO-style workloads \citep{ji2025tree}. Workspace forking (ForkRL) and per-turn SWE snapshots (SPROUT) follow the same direction\unverified. The consequence is that rollout branching has become a credit-assignment estimator in its own right, and its cost ranges from nearly free (math+Python) to infrastructure-bound (SWE, web).

\paragraph{Where credit becomes the bottleneck}
These difficulties compound. Outcome-only reward dilutes per-turn signal in proportion to horizon. Tool-induced drift and the Echo Trap make the resulting gradients noisy or degenerate. SimpleTIR traces harmful negative samples to ``compounding errors induced by using external tool outputs''. Once stability is under control, the remaining question is which turn caused the outcome. This is the stated motivation of the turn-level methods of 2025, MT-GRPO \citep{zeng2025reinforcing}, GiGPO \citep{feng2025group}, SPA-RL \citep{wang2025spa} and Tree-GRPO \citep{ji2025tree}, and of roughly a dozen 2026 preprints on leave-one-turn, information-gain and rubric-attributed turn rewards\unverified. These methods form a crowded field with no standard benchmark or comparison protocol. Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy} organizes them.
